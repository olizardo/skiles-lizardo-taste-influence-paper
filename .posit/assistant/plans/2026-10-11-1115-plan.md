## Part A — Correctness fixes (apply in all scenarios)

**A1. §4.2: Ruskin–Whistler trial date (factual error).**
"a tension that famously provoked the 1877 Ruskin-Whistler libel trial" → "a tension that famously provoked the 1878 Whistler–Ruskin libel trial". (Ruskin's review appeared in 1877 and Whistler filed suit that year, but the trial was held 25–26 November 1878, with the farthing verdict.)

**A2. §3.1: "raw evaluations" mislabel.**
"raw evaluations increase from 4.65 to 4.85 (+0.203, p < 0.01)" — these are the **IPW-weighted** trial means (the unweighted means are 4.49→4.69). "Raw" invites misreading as unweighted descriptive means. Fix: drop "raw" → "evaluations increase from 4.65 to 4.85"; likewise "reducing second-trial evaluations to a flat −0.030" already reads fine.

**A3. §3.2: colloquial phrasing.**
"Beyond the one shining spot mentioned, the Same-Status Alignment (Hypothesis 3) had a rough time with these data and study design, receiving little to no support..." → formal academic register, e.g.: "Apart from this isolated instance, Hypothesis 3 (Same-Status Alignment) receives little to no support from these data, especially for information about others' *positive* aesthetic judgments (likes)." (Preserves the same citations and figures.)

**A4. §3.3: overstated opening of the reactance paragraph.**
"...non-college-educated working-class individuals are *more* likely to react against others' tastes than individuals in the other three status-consistency categories" — the Middle/No-College contrast is only marginal (AME = −0.018, p = 0.08). Soften the opener to "than individuals in the college-educated categories", keeping the existing nuanced sentences that follow unchanged.

**A5. §3.2: unshown analysis claim ("ancillary tests") — DECIDED: add appendix backing.**
"Ancillary tests confirm that terminal Associate degree holders (N = 196) and non-degreed college attenders (N = 459) are equally likely to use 'staying' as the dominant response" — no table or appendix backs this. Plan:
  1. At execution time, derive the tier-2 split and stay rates from the pipeline data (terminal AA vs. non-degreed attenders within the Associate/Some College tier, primary sample) and run a formal comparison of stay rates (e.g., chi-square or proportion test).
  2. Add a one-sentence note to Appendix C (after the paragraph introducing Table A.4, or at the end of the section) reporting the split Ns, the two stay rates, and the test result.
  3. Keep the §3.2 sentence but point to the appendix ("...are equally likely to use 'staying' as the dominant response; see Appendix \ref{sec:appendix_regression_tables}").
  Note: the audit verified the 196/459 split Ns but not the stay-rate comparison itself, so step 1 must confirm the claim holds before wording is finalized. If the comparison fails, revert to softening the in-text claim instead.
